Architecture Philosophy

Architecture starts before solutioning.

I do not begin with a platform, screen or predetermined technology. I begin by understanding what the organisation is trying to achieve, how the service currently operates, what capabilities are required, what information moves through the process and which systems or services already support those needs.

The resulting solution is therefore shaped across business, operations, information and technology rather than optimising one interface in isolation.

Solution Analysis & Architecture Shaping

How I analyse a solution before deciding what to build.

My current work involves analysing solutions across product, capability, process, people, information, APIs and services, systems, controls and operational dependencies.

The objective is to understand what already exists, where capability gaps sit, what can be reused, where integration is required and what genuinely needs to change.

Architecture Analysis Method

Business need → technical responsibility

I use capability mapping, object modelling, process analysis and API/service assessment to separate the business problem from the proposed implementation.

Business Capability Process Actor Object Service System Dependency Solution Option
Capability Mapping

Identify what the business must be able to do, determine whether the capability already exists and establish where ownership should sit.

Object Modelling

Model the information entities involved, their relationships, lifecycle and ownership before mapping them to systems.

API & Service Analysis

Review existing APIs, services and platform capabilities to determine behaviour, dependencies, reuse opportunities and capability gaps.

Process & People Analysis

Understand who performs the work, where hand-offs occur, where decisions are made and where operational friction is created.

Solution Option Evaluation

Determine whether the appropriate response is process change, capability reuse, platform configuration, service reuse, integration, automation, new technical capability or a combination of these.
